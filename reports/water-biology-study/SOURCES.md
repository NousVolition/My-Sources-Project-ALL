# Evidence and provenance

Accessed 2026-10-09. Published evidence is separate from this package's simulations. No new empirical water samples or raw experimental freezing-event dataset was analyzed.

| ID | Primary source | What is supported / what is not imported |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | [Wex et al. (2015), ACP 15, 1463–1485](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/15/1463/2015/), DOI 10.5194/acp-15-1463-2015 | Seven-instrument comparison of ice activity from nonviable bacterial reference material. Supports viability-independent biological nucleation; does not imply all bacteria or environmental waters share its activity. |
| S2 | [Budke & Koop (2015), AMT 8, 689–703](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/689/2015/), DOI 10.5194/amt-8-689-2015 | Equation (9), Appendix A1 probability equation, and Table A2 on printed page 700. PDF text and rendered table inspected. Six values manually transcribed in `data/published_budke_koop_table_A2.csv`; source CC BY 3.0. Reported uncertainties kept as ± quantities, not silently converted into confidence intervals. Class A's local omega is used as a slope, not a complete calibrated CNT parameterization. Class C's omega and lambda differ and are not interchanged. |
| S3 | [Rusconi, Guasto & Stocker (2014), Nature Physics 10, 212–217](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys2883), DOI 10.1038/nphys2883 | Measured shear trapping of motile bacteria through shape/alignment and orientation noise. Supports mechanisms beyond passive dye transport. Our simplified imposed-swimming control omits the alignment model and does not reproduce this experiment. |
| S4 | [Ran et al. (2021), PNAS 118, e2108548118](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2108548118); [open author version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01049) | Experiments show flow/motility coupling can change scalar transport, with different effects by spatial scale. Not a parameter calibration for dilute ordinary water in this pilot. |
| S5 | [Beydoun, Polen & Sullivan (2017), ACP 17, 13545–13557](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/17/13545/2017/), DOI 10.5194/acp-17-13545-2017 | Biological/mineral mixture study motivates component and mixture controls. Our synthetic hazards are not fits to these experimental curves. |
| S6 | [Addula et al. (2024), ACP 24, 10833](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/10833/2024/), DOI 10.5194/acp-24-10833-2024 | Survival formalism makes volume and cooling history explicit. No homogeneous-nucleation prefactor from this paper is extrapolated to bacteria or ordinary household water. |
| S7 | [Bacterial floc mediated rapid streamer formation in creeping flows (2015), Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13070), DOI 10.1038/srep13070 | Primary observations of floc attachment and shear deformation into streamers. Used as a mechanism example, not as a source for aggregation rates or a wet-lab protocol. |

Classical modeling relations used without species-specific calibration: Fickian advection–diffusion; Stokes–Einstein diffusivity for a sphere; Stokes terminal settling at low Reynolds number; dilute passive-sphere Einstein viscosity; incompressible Boussinesq perturbation equations; conditional Poisson survival; dimensional capillary, heat-capacity and D²-law sensitivities. Assumed radii, density differences, material-property shifts, stochastic frailty distributions and absolute synthetic hazard scales are all scenario inputs, not values attributed to those papers.

The source project's root README, `reports/fluid-organization-pilot/README.md`, and the first 150 lines of `core.py` were inspected through the connected GitHub account. Its repository tree was inspected recursively, with no AGENTS.md found. The transport code here is new, standalone NumPy code rather than a silent modification of the existing Navier–Stokes solver. The projectless workspace's minimal Git runtime lacked an HTTPS remote helper, so repository publication uses the connected GitHub API.
