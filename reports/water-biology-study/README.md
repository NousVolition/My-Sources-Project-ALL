# Water, suspended biological material, and freezing: three-question pilot

**Completed computational companion to NousVolition/My-Sources-Project-ALL. No living cultures, new laboratory measurements, or new physical law.** The calculations distinguish dissolved solute, suspended particles, density/temperature buoyancy, and stochastic ice nucleation. Numerical validation verifies the specified models; it does not validate an unspecified sample of bacteria-bearing water.

The short answer is **yes: bacteria-bearing water and ordinary water are miscible**. Their water molecules do not form mutually repelling liquid phases merely because bacteria are present. Advection, molecular diffusion, density and temperature differences, and stratification govern physical mixing. Bacterial cells are suspended particulates: they have much smaller Brownian diffusivity than a dissolved dye and may attach, settle, aggregate, or swim. Water can mix while particles remain patchy or collect on a surface. No component needs to lead, compete for the front, or choose to repel another component.

Living bacteria, dead cells, extracellular ice-nucleating proteins or fragments, and pathogenicity are different categories. Motility and metabolism require appropriate living organisms and conditions. Dead cells and extracellular material can retain physical surfaces or ice-nucleating activity. Ice nucleation is not evidence of life or pathogenicity, and a lack of ice nucleation is not evidence of microbial safety. Not every bacterial species or cell is an efficient ice nucleator.

## Coupled material-feedback follow-up

The additional [coupled response study](coupled-feedback/README.md) is complete: **30 paired comparisons, 60 trajectories**, with material transport feeding back through an assumed concentration-dependent viscosity. Rearranging equal amounts of material changes the disturbance-specific velocity response by **0.31–0.43% of the initial impulse** in the time-RMS metric across four starting phases. Feedback-off and uniform-material controls give zero contrast; representative grid and timestep checks pass. This establishes a conditional effect in the stated model, not a measured biological coefficient, a history-dependent force or a bifurcation. The original three-question outputs below remain the original package; the follow-up has its own protocol, six tests, data, charts and reproducibility checks.

### Q1: How rapidly do they mix, and do particles separate?

In the periodic, externally prescribed stirring model, the dissolved-solute variance fell by **99.9943% on average after eight model seconds**. Across eight independently phased flows, the mean's seed-bootstrap 95% interval was **99.9916–99.9965%**. The first recorded time to 95% variance reduction ranged from **2.6 to 3.2 s**, with 0.1 s recording resolution. These times belong to the stated millimetre-scale model, not all cups, clouds, or natural water.

With the same diffusivity but no flow, variance fell by **46.83%** in eight seconds. The analytic 95% time is **37.94 s** for this periodic sinusoidal starting profile. A label for a source water is not an immiscible phase; the initial profile is deliberately smooth and partially overlapping, not a sharp interface.

At an assumed diameter of 1 micrometre, 293.15 K, and viscosity 0.001 Pa s, Stokes–Einstein diffusion gives **4.29 × 10⁻¹³ m²/s** for passive spheres. The chosen dissolved tracer has **1 × 10⁻⁹ m²/s**, about **2,329 times larger**. In eight seconds, the one-coordinate diffusion-only RMS displacements are **2.62 micrometres versus 126.49 micrometres**. Both are calculated scale estimates, not measurements of a bacterial species.

After stirring, inert-particle overlap with a uniform distribution was **0.9277** (seed-bootstrap 95% interval **0.9259–0.9296**) on a fixed 16 × 16 observation grid with 8,192 particles. The noise-corrected coarse concentration CV² was **0.00198** (interval **0.00030–0.00329**). Brownian and assumed motile controls have similar final coarse overlap; this short pilot does not establish a robust biological enhancement. Finite particle sampling itself limits measured overlap. Counts of 2,048, 8,192 and 32,768 were compared. A finely filamented, nondiffusive particle distribution can look uniform in coarse bins while remaining unmixed at finer scales.

A separate quiescent one-millimetre column uses a **50 kg/m³ excess density**, spherical particles, Stokes drag and an absorbing bottom. For diameter 1 micrometre, the calculated settling speed is **0.02725 micrometres/s**, or **98.1 micrometres/hour**; the expected deposited fraction of an initially uniform column is **9.81% in one hour**. Sixteen independent placements quantify sampling variation. A diameter-10-micrometre inert control settles 100 times faster in this ideal model. That larger-particle control illustrates a possible consequence of aggregation; it does **not** simulate aggregate formation. A separate assumed attachment rate transfers particles into an attached compartment, with suspended + attached + deposited counts exactly conserved. Brownian diffusion, resuspension, complex cell shape and resolved wall encounters are absent from this column model.

Stable stratification suppresses vertical displacement, while an inverted density gradient can drive buoyant motion. Both signs of a mean density gradient and a mean thermal gradient are tested with Boussinesq equations. These are dimensionless sensitivity calculations with separately stated viscosity and diffusivities, not the same physical scaling as the stirring example. See the exact final estimates in `summary.json`. The initially underresolved unstable-density run is retained in the convergence table and replaced by a finer primary calculation. Apparent failure to mix can therefore arise from ordinary physical layering without biological repulsion.

Other mechanisms have different signatures. Recirculating flow can retain particles within slowly exchanging regions; buoyant or settling particles drift relative to the water; motile elongated cells can redistribute through flow-induced alignment [S3]. Attachment transfers material to an interface rather than destroying it. Aggregation requires collisions and sticking: extracellular polymers can bind cells and particles, while shear can promote encounters, stretch structures, or break them. Experiments with bacterial flocs show attachment and stretching into streamers [S7]. Those mechanisms depend on surface chemistry, ionic conditions, size and shape, and are not implied merely by seeing cloudy water. Our particle clustering statistic measures spatial nonuniformity, not physical bonds or floc formation.

### Q2: Can biological components change the response to stress?

Yes, under appropriate conditions, but adding a small number of passive cells does not automatically change the bulk fluid appreciably. The completed passive-sphere comparison uses volume fraction phi and the dilute Einstein relation **mu_eff / mu = 1 + 2.5 phi**. At the illustrative concentration **10⁶ spheres/mL** and diameter 1 micrometre, phi is **5.24 × 10⁻⁷**, giving a viscosity increase of **1.31 parts per million**. The table distinguishes fixed shear rate, where stress changes, from fixed stress, where shear rate changes. This is a passive dilute estimate; rod shape, active stresses and concentrated suspensions can violate its assumptions.

Completed property ablations also calculate capillary number mu U/sigma, inertial capillary time proportional to sigma⁻¹/², a lumped thermal response time proportional to heat capacity at fixed conductance, and a diffusion-limited droplet D²-law lifetime proportional to 1/K. For example, an **assumed 10% surface-tension reduction** increases capillary number by **11.11%** and capillary time by **5.41%**. An **assumed 10% larger evaporation coefficient** reduces the model lifetime by **9.09%**. These are algebraic sensitivities, not observed bacterial effects or resolved droplet formation, heating or evaporation simulations. Humidity, airflow, geometry and external temperature are held fixed in those comparisons.

Biological activity may matter through secreted surfactants, extracellular polymers and biofilms, changes in chemistry or density caused by metabolism, surface-attached material, particle loading, and active swimming stresses. These mechanisms require measured constitutive parameters and relevant concentration/time scales. Living motile suspensions have experimentally altered transport and mixing under specific conditions [S3, S4]. Thus a generally weak dilute passive correction is not a universal upper bound on living systems. The original one-way particle model cannot demonstrate feedback on the water. The separate [coupled follow-up](coupled-feedback/README.md) now tests passive concentration-to-viscosity feedback; measured active rheology, chemistry and biofilm growth remain extensions.

### Q3: Can biological ice nucleators change freezing probability?

Published experiments support this possibility, including ice-active material from nonviable bacteria [S1]. Our completed calculation uses the established stochastic survival relation **S(t) = exp[-integral h(T(t)) dt]** and conditional interval probability **1 - exp(-integral h dt)**. Independent bulk, mineral, biological and surface contributions add to the hazard under the model's independence assumption. Measurements should compare survival curves and rate distributions, not a deterministic temperature assigned to a water label.

The published class-A temperature sensitivity **8.7 ± 0.4 K⁻¹** [S2, Table A2] informs one local rate slope. With this constant-slope approximation, doubling cooling rate shifts the median colder by **0.0797 K**; the reported-slope sensitivity range is **0.0762–0.0835 K colder**. This is a model-derived shift using a published parameter, not a newly measured effect or a universal prediction. The ±0.4 is retained as reported, not relabeled as a 95% confidence interval. The six published table entries are transcribed with source location; they are aggregate parameters, not independent raw droplets.

The synthetic survival study uses **14 arms × 12 independent simulated batches × 96 droplets = 16,128 synthetic droplets**. It includes lognormal batch and droplet variation, surface/mineral/bio ablations, and volume/cooling controls. Every absolute rate scale and reference offset is explicitly assumed. **The horizontal axis is cooling below an unspecified reference temperature; no absolute Celsius freezing onset is predicted.** “Distilled”, “filtered”, “heated”, “boiled” and “untreated” are matched-physics null labels with identical underlying hazards, not fabricated estimates of those real waters. Their random sample curves differ through sampling variation. Adding the assumed biological hazard shifts synthetic curves earlier by construction; the independent evidence for a biological effect comes from the cited experiments, not that construction.

The synthetic fraction frozen by a selected relative cooling of 5 K is about **0.9%** in one matched-null arm versus **9.9%** in the mineral scenario. Increasing only the assumed container contribution also shifts freezing. Those contrasts demonstrate confounding sensitivity. The deliberately strong biological scenario has essentially saturated by this endpoint, so its pointwise bootstrap band collapses to 1; that is **not proof that true probability is exactly 1**. Median distributions and the full transition curves are more informative there. No real-water treatment ranking is established, and no raw experimental droplet dataset was reanalyzed.

For Q1, the primary particle comparison holds initial positions and flow phases matched within a seed. Different seeds are independent model realizations, not independent biological samples. The no-flow solute result repeats identically for each seed because its equation has no random inputs; its zero bootstrap width is not experimental certainty. The primary stratification ensemble contains four phase seeds, which gives only a small numerical sensitivity sample. Representative seed 200 receives grid/time refinement for each stratification mechanism/sign; representative seed 100 receives scalar refinement. Neither covers all possible initial conditions.

For Q3, droplets are nested in independent simulated batches. Uncertainty resamples whole batches (4,000 replicates for summaries; 2,000 for pointwise survival bands), not all droplets as independent experimental batches. Bands are pointwise, not simultaneous. The chosen batch variation is an assumption, so uncertainty quantifies Monte Carlo variation conditional on the model; it excludes uncertainty in uncalibrated rate coefficients. No significance claim is made from exploratory contrasts. For a future empirical study, choose sample size through a prespecified detectable shift and pilot-based power or precision analysis, with independent lots and run days, not just repeated measurements of the same droplets.

## Treatments are not microbial-only interventions

Distilled water, filtered water, warmed/cooled water, boiled water and untreated water change more than one variable and cannot simply be labeled “no bacteria” versus “bacteria.” Distillation changes ions and nonvolatile material; handling can recontaminate it. Filtration depends on the actual pore rating, membrane, integrity, adsorption, size distribution and pressure; cells and minerals may be removed together while dissolved ions and small extracellular nucleators can pass. A nominal **0.2 micrometre pore size must be recorded, never treated as a guarantee that every biological nucleator or contaminant is absent**. Heated water is not synonymous with sterilized water. Boiling can change dissolved gases, carbonate equilibria, precipitates, concentration through water loss, proteins and particle surfaces. Cooling and storage allow further changes and recontamination. None of these treatments determines pathogenicity by itself.

The future sample record must include: source and lot, certified viability status, particle size/count/composition, dissolved ions and conductivity, pH, organic content, filtration pore size and membrane, complete heating/cooling and storage history, evaporation loss, final sample volume, container material/coating/roughness and contact area, air/water-interface area, temperature calibration and thermal lag, actual per-sample cooling profile, humidity, airflow and frost/neighbor-seeding checks. Samples with unrecorded conditions cannot support microbial causal attribution.

An attribution design would pair aliquots from one characterized base water across blank, sham, inert particle, mineral-only, nonviable biological material-only, and mineral-plus-biological arms; include treatment blanks and reagent controls; and cross container and cooling conditions. Match ionic composition and total nonbiological particle burden or measure them and model their effects. Heat sensitivity alone is suggestive, not uniquely biological: independent compositional evidence and particle/surface controls are needed. Separating extracellular material from intact cells requires validated size/chemical characterization, not assuming that filtering or killing removes ice activity. Container, mineral and bio hazards cannot generally be uniquely identified from a single survival curve.

**Optional human-operated validation only:** an appropriately overseen institutional facility could compare well-characterized inert particles and certified safe, nonviable ice-nucleating reference material under its approved procedures. This package supplies no culturing, environmental isolation, enrichment, or pathogen amplification protocol. Do not culture environmental or unknown bacteria for this study. No wet-lab work has been performed or authorized by these computations.

## Equations, measurements and numerical scope

The scalar equation is `dc/dt + u.grad(c) = D Lap(c)` with `div(u)=0`. Q1 uses a 1 × 1 periodic box, alternating `u=(sin(2*pi*z+phase),0)` and `u=(0,sin(2*pi*x+phase))`, each for half a time unit. Exact Fourier translations along the active shear and exact Fourier diffusion are combined by symmetric splitting. Steps land on every flow switch. Grid 64², step 0.01 and D=0.001 map illustratively to L=1 mm, U=1 mm/s, D=10⁻⁹ m²/s; stirring must be externally imposed. A 2D dye concentration is a continuum source fraction; numerical particle count is a sampling budget, not a claim of a particular 3D microbial concentration.

Particle transport is overdamped one-way advection plus separately switchable Brownian increments. The optional swimming term is an **imposed active-Brownian control**, speed 20 micrometres/s, angular diffusivity 1/s. Its orientation is externally randomized; it omits vorticity rotation, rod alignment, chemotaxis, and cell–cell interactions. It is not a faithful model of a named organism or the shear-trapping experiments. The inert and Brownian controls do not use invented biological forces. Shear exposure integrates the analytic scalar shear rate along each trajectory; the saved per-particle distribution includes mean and 95th percentile. No stress-induced damage model is inferred from exposure.

Definitions: `variance = mean((c-mean(c))²)`; `mixing = 1-variance/variance_initial`; water-source overlap `mean(2 min(c,1-c))`; normalized distribution overlap `1-0.5 sum(abs(p-q))`; particle `CV² = 256 sum(p_bin²)-1`. The uniform multinomial noise floor is 255/N; subtracting it can produce small negative estimates and is not clipped. Solute/particle spatial overlap is additionally recorded for seed 100 in common 16² bins, using approximate block integration of the scalar. This overlap is descriptive and bin-dependent, not a biological mechanism test.

The stratification solver uses dealiased Fourier 2D Boussinesq perturbation equations about a linear mean gradient: momentum has buoyancy `b e_z`; buoyancy evolves with `-N² w` and its own diffusivity. N²=4, 0, -4 represent stable, neutral and unstable gradients. Pressure projection enforces incompressibility. Mean gradients are imposed and total background is not periodic; perturbations are. Positive N² permits the stable total-energy check `K + mean(b²)/(2N²)`. Diffusion is integrated exactly around RK4 transport/buoyancy stages; overall temporal order is two. Thermal diffusivity differs from compositional diffusivity, so thermal and density perturbations need not persist equally. The linear equation of state excludes water's density maximum near 4 °C, freezing, latent heat and phase boundaries. This nondimensional comparison has nu=0.01 and D=0.001 and is not quantitatively calibrated to ordinary water's Schmidt number.

The scalar analytic checks cover pure diffusion, translation with diffusion, and a nonuniform shear with a known transported cosine. Final scalar grid differences fall from **2.12 × 10⁻⁵** (32²→64²) to **1.58 × 10⁻⁹** (64²→128²) in RMS concentration for the checked seed. Step-halving differences are **1.02 × 10⁻⁶** and **2.56 × 10⁻⁷**, giving observed order **2.00**. Maximum scalar mean error is **2.33 × 10⁻¹⁵**. Budgets, positivity, Brownian MSD, Stokes deposition, incompressibility, stable-energy decay, hazard integration and Monte Carlo survival all have automated checks. Error gates are numerical screens, not a proof of continuum convergence. The failed coarse unstable-density comparison is preserved, and its refined results must meet the separate saved-data checks before figures are accepted.

## Relation to Navier–Stokes and the existing project

The existing [fluid-organization pilot](https://github.com/NousVolition/My-Sources-Project-ALL/tree/main/reports/fluid-organization-pilot) used incompressible 3D Navier–Stokes and one-way fog/smoke particles, while listing phase change as future work. This companion adds a deliberately smaller, validated transport problem and a separate nucleation-statistics layer. It does not replace that solver or interpret its markers as molecules. The repository was inspected at commit `8e1525f64b27909afb6dcf704848b8666ad2260a`; exact publication ancestry is recorded separately.

Navier–Stokes governs continuum momentum. It does not by itself specify species diffusivity, particle drift/attachment/coagulation, swimmer orientation or active stresses, chemistry, surface tension, evaporation, droplet formation, or stochastic ice nucleation. Those require extra transport equations, boundary conditions, constitutive laws and independently measured parameters. A dilute one-way approximation may be appropriate when momentum feedback is negligible. Variable-density/temperature feedback is represented here only through Boussinesq terms; strongly variable density or phase change requires more.

Clouds and fog contain liquid droplets or ice in air, not a second kind of molecular water. Airborne cells or biological fragments can enter droplets and sometimes act as nucleators. Ordinary evaporation produces molecular water vapor; microbial transport through splashing, entrained droplets or aerosols is a different process. This pilot does not simulate aerosol generation, cloud population dynamics or fire. Nothing here settles the Navier–Stokes existence/smoothness question or establishes a new fluid law.

## Reproduce and audit

Use Python 3.12 and install `requirements.txt` in a fresh environment (the exact run versions are in `requirements-lock.txt` and `environment.json`). From this directory:

```text
python run_study.py
python audit_transport.py
python analyze.py
python -m pytest test_study.py -q --junitxml=tests.xml
python make_report.py
python verify_package.py
```

For independent output without overwriting the delivered raw results, run `python run_study.py --out reproduction`, then compare its tables/arrays to `data/`. The analysis script reads the delivered folder by default; copy the source package into another directory to rerender a complete independent report. All random generators have explicit independent seeds/streams. Results are deterministic for a pinned software environment up to floating-point and plotting differences. Numeric arrays, not ZIP timestamps or PNG metadata, are the reproducibility target.

`run_study.py` writes configurations, raw maps, binned particle histories, initial/final particle positions, individual cumulative exposures, budgets, per-droplet events and summary estimates. Intermediate particle positions between initial and final snapshots are not archived; their bin counts and diagnostics are saved every 0.1 time unit and full paths can be regenerated from the seeds. All eight solute trajectories have raw diagnostic tables; full concentration maps are retained for the representative seed in each flow. Refined comparison runs retain diagnostics/errors rather than every fine-grid trajectory. No data have been silently relabeled as laboratory measurements.

`manifest.json` records SHA-256 hashes of delivered files. `verify_package.py` checks those hashes and numeric invariants. The source/evidence record distinguishes the transcribed published table from synthetic data. The original conversation's user questions were retrieved; its older assistant responses remained opaque content-reference placeholders, so no missing scientific claims from them were assumed.

An independent full rerun reproduced all **48 regenerated raw-data files**: tables compared byte-for-byte and numeric arrays at rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-13. Thirteen automated tests passed. The separate `audit_transport.py` also checks scalar concentration errors throughout the saved time history and the recorded homogenization time for seed 100, so a small final-field error cannot conceal a transient discrepancy; see `transport-audit.json`. SHA checks, these numerical screens and independent reproduction assess different aspects of reliability.
