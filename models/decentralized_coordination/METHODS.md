# Model and analysis specification

## Research question and status

The question is whether local coordination with the option to decline can produce agreement, and whether apparent influence follows network position or individual identity. All current records are generated by code. Probabilities below are stipulated, not estimated from behavior. The simulator provides testable mechanisms and counterexamples; empirical claims need an independently conducted human study.

## Primary design

Nine vertices form an undirected cycle: seat `s` sees `(s−1) mod 9` and `(s+1) mod 9`. There is no endpoint and no higher-degree seat. Colors at time zero are independent Bernoulli(.5), including the two unanimous patterns, whose probability is 2/512. Each matched block draws a fresh color pattern, a random permutation of the nine identities and a random A leader. Those attributes are held fixed for A/B/C within the block. Between blocks they are redrawn. All six condition orders occur in shuffled cycles of six blocks; 4,000 blocks leave four orders with one extra repetition. Every execution order is saved.

Pilot: four matched blocks, 12 rounds, separate seed namespace. Main: 4,000 matched blocks, 12,000 rounds. The four-round pilot does not cover all orders or all identity/position pairs. Main Monte Carlo rounds reset memory; order randomization in that model cannot itself demonstrate absence of human learning or carryover.

The clock runs for at most 120 simulated seconds. Initial unanimity succeeds at zero. First unanimity is absorbing because the experiment stops there, even if the underlying C rule would later leave consensus. Success at exactly 120 is an event; failure at 120 is censored. No extrapolation beyond this horizon is made for C.

## A: fixed hierarchy

The leader commands their own initial color and holds that command for the entire round. At seconds 10, 20, …, 120, each differing follower changes with probability `compliance`, independently. The primary value 1 is an idealized obedient benchmark; .7 and .3 are sensitivity values. Followers see a global command, so A differs from B/C in information availability as well as authority. The leader's initial color policy is a chosen implementation of a fixed directive, not an inferred human strategy.

## B: the exact deterministic local rule

At seconds 10, 20, …, 120, every seat uses the same pre-update snapshot. Switch iff both neighbors show the opposite color; otherwise hold. This is equivalent to majority among the seat and its two neighbors, so ties do not arise. Computing updates in place would change the specified model and is tested against an independent simultaneous majority oracle.

B contains no water molecules, interactions, temperature, fluid equations, energy or mass transport. “Water-inspired” refers only to the analogy of local interactions without a designated leader. No equivalence to water or molecular dynamics is asserted.

Exact enumeration follows all 512 states to first recurrence, giving the transient and attractor period. All end at a fixed point within four updates: 152 reach unanimity, 360 remain in disagreement. Consensus times are 0 s: 2 starts; 10 s: 60; 20 s: 54; 30 s: 18; 40 s: 18. There are zero longer-period attractors for this nine-seat ring under this rule. This is a finite exhaustive computation, not a claim about arbitrary graph sizes or other update schedules.

A useful invariant explains freezing: two adjacent seats of the same color each always have at least one same-color neighbor, so neither can switch. Their mutual protection persists. If the initial state contains both a red adjacent pair and a blue adjacent pair, unanimity is impossible for B. Temporary flip-backs before settling are possible even though sustained cycles are absent.

## C: stipulated voluntary-choice mechanism

Primary C has no leader and only local color information. At each integer second, shuffle all nine seats. Each, independently, has an opportunity to act with probability .25. Active seats act once in that second in shuffled order, seeing immediately preceding updates. This is a **one-second discrete asynchronous approximation**, not a continuous-time process and not an implementation of a human decision rate learned from data. Consensus is checked after every active decision and assigned that integer second.

For an active seat:

1. With probability `innovation=.02`, initiate by flipping its own color. This branch is a chosen action and has no additional refusal coin.
2. Otherwise select one visible neighbor, uniformly in homogeneous runs. The neighbor's current color is an implicit proposal.
3. If the proposed color is the current color, hold; this is **not refusal**.
4. If the proposed color differs, decline with probability `refusal=.15`, otherwise switch.

No communication of intentions occurs. Only this model knows which proposal was declined or which neighbor was selected. Humans might use different rules, attend to multiple neighbors, misunderstand displays, wait strategically, care about relationships, or value dissent. The code does not model or adjudicate those possibilities.

Sensitivity: refusal in {0, .15, .5, .9} × innovation in {0, .02, .1}; a no-switch boundary with refusal 1 and innovation 0; activation .08 and .6; a simultaneous-10-second variant with **everyone active at each tick**; a heterogeneous identity profile; former-leader attention weights 3 and 6. Activation is unused in `sync10`; this sensitivity changes timing and opportunities jointly, so it does not isolate synchrony by itself. Each scenario uses the same 1,200 starting blocks. Seeds are matched across scenarios but branch-dependent draw consumption means these are not perfectly coupled counterfactual random tapes. The primary main sample is independent of the sensitivity sample.

In the heterogeneous sensitivity, identity 0 is four times as likely to be selected *when among visible neighbors*, declines a differing proposal with probability .65, and initiates with probability .03. Other identities retain primary settings. This bundles three identity traits; it does not estimate their separate effects. The privileged identity is an arbitrary label; exchanging labels with the profile would exchange the advantage.

## Identity, position and persistence

The primary ring is vertex-transitive: no seat has a structural advantage before the local color configuration is considered. Local configuration can still break symmetry within a round. Merely permuting people around that ring does not create a topology contrast.

A separate C-only experiment crosses ring versus star and homogeneous versus heterogeneous profiles. The star has hub seat 0 with eight neighbors and eight leaves with one neighbor. This tests altered degree and information access jointly; its result cannot be attributed to a single centrality statistic. B is never run on the star because “both immediate neighbors” would be undefined at leaves.

For each of 240 independently generated starting-state replicates, draw a random identity ordering and all nine cyclic shifts in shuffled execution order. Each identity occupies every seat exactly once per replicate. Hold colors fixed by seat across the nine mappings. Dynamic random seeds differ across mappings but are shared across topology/profile scenarios. There are 4 × 240 × 9 = 8,640 rounds and 77,760 agent observations. The replicate, not an agent row, is the independent unit. This balanced crossing removes a permanent identity/seat confound; matched starts also mean successive rounds are dependent.

The model records **copy-source credits**: an actual switch to a selected neighbor's color credits that neighbor by 1. A follower adopting A's command credits the designated leader. A B switch credits each of the two agreeing neighbors by .5. An autonomous C initiative gives no credit to an external source. Credit totals therefore equal switches minus initiatives. These credits are mechanism attribution inside the simulator; co-occurring human card changes do not reveal such credits.

Per topology/profile, fit an additive descriptive decomposition of copied-switch counts into grand mean + identity effect + seat effect. The fully balanced design makes the main effects orthogonal. Report variance fractions for identity and seat, with all remaining variation called unexplained (including interactions, initial configuration and stochastic variation). Do not label that remainder noise alone. For predictive evaluation, fit on the first 80% of full replicates and evaluate on the remaining 20%. Prediction gain is `1 − MSE(model)/MSE(training-mean baseline)` on held-out rows. Negative gain is allowed. This is a single preregistered deterministic split and not an uncertainty interval or a significance test.

Within a replicate, compare top copy-source identities and seats across successive shuffled mappings. Ties are split uniformly; a tie set of sizes m and n contributes overlap/(m×n). Skip pairs where either round has no copied switch. The independent exchangeable reference is 1/9, but matched states violate independence, so no p-value is inferred from that reference. Persistence of a top copy source is a descriptive proxy, not proof of continuing authority.

The former-leader sensitivity uses the same hypothetical individual designated for A in that block. If A occurs before C (even with B in between), multiply that identity's neighbor-selection weight by 3 or 6 in C. Otherwise apply no multiplier. Baseline weight 1 implements **no status memory**. Former-leader share is defined only after A and when there is at least one credited switch; its denominator and missing counts are saved. No status update, decay, remembered relations, or learning is inferred. Persistence rises only if the inserted mechanism produces it; this does not demonstrate that people obey former leaders.

## Outcome dictionary

| Field | Definition and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| `consensus`, `consensus_time` | Whether all nine colors ever match before stopping; time is null/blank on timeout. |
| `capped_time` | min(first consensus time, 120); mean includes timeouts at 120. It is not the mean uncensored consensus time. |
| `switches`, `switches_by_id` | Actual color changes before stopping. |
| `first_switch_ids` | All identities changing at the earliest recorded second; ties retained. This is an observational initiation proxy. |
| `first_initiative_ids` | C's first explicitly modeled autonomous flips; not necessarily the first switches. |
| `refusals`, `refusals_by_id` | Rejected differing proposals under A/C; none under deterministic B. Holding a matching color is excluded. |
| `differing_proposals` | Proposed changes, both accepted and rejected, including autonomous initiatives. The reported refusal ratio uses this explicitly defined denominator; it is not exactly the C refusal parameter. |
| `adjacent_tick_reversals` | Seats switching on consecutive observation ticks (1 s for primary C, 10 s for A/B). Binary colors imply flip-back. Not comparable as a time-normalized rate across schedules. |
| `state_revisits` | An end-of-tick changed global state previously seen, excluding immediate unchanged holds. Stochastic revisits are not deterministic limit cycles. |
| `copy_hhi` | Sum of squared shares of copy-source credits; null if no credits. High concentration is not automatically authority. |
| `former_leader_share` | Former leader's share of credited switches, when former leader is defined and total credits > 0. |

Only pilots keep every event; main/sensitivity aggregate by round. All runs retain initial/final state, mapping, seed derivation and necessary source information for exact replay. Integer timestamps can tie within C's shuffled sequence; `events` preserves actual execution order for pilots. No unique leader is invented to break a tie.

## Statistics and limitations

Consensus proportions have 95% Wilson Monte Carlo intervals within a scenario. Matched A−B, C−B and A−C contrasts use 1,500 bootstrap draws over entire starting-state blocks. The simulations involve nine labels, not nine independent human participants. These intervals quantify sampling of model starts and random choices, not uncertainty about whether the model is true. Pilot records are excluded from all main contrasts.

Time-limit failures are not dropped. Report success fraction and mean capped time together; success-only medians are explicitly conditional and can be misleading when success rates differ. Color unanimity says nothing about satisfaction, creativity, fairness, survival, consent or enduring cohesion. Freedom to refuse removes any unconditional guarantee of consensus: all members can choose to hold a nonunanimous initial configuration. A possibility result under a nonzero refusal probability is not a guarantee for all voluntary policies.

A is advantaged by centralized information and stipulated compliance. B's strict rule can trap the group. C has different opportunities, randomness, and initiative. A/B/C outcome contrasts bundle these mechanisms and do not identify a pure causal effect of freedom. Degree, visibility, timing, starting configurations, identities, repeated exposure and group-level dependence must be addressed in any human extension. No claim proves or disproves Freud, and no Hénon-map calculation or fluid-physics inference is part of this study.
